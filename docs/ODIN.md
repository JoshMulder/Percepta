# ODIN — the command-centre overview

> Design produced 2026-08-14 by a 13-agent survey/design/judge/synthesis pass over this
> repository. Three designs were scored independently: **ODIN — the Operations Wall** 31/40, **ODIN Watch Desk — a queue-first alert triage console** 31/40, **ODIN Watch — a guard list, a talk log, and context on demand** 30/40.
> What follows is the winner's spine with the other two grafted in and the judges' fatal
> flaws fixed. Decisions taken by the product owner are recorded under each question.

## What Odin is

ODIN is built on design #1's spine — the Operations Wall: one full-screen surface an operator does not touch, where colour is rationed to mean attention, sort order is itself an alarm, and drill-in is a drawer that never costs you the wall. Three grafts from the other designs and five verified fixes make it shippable.

Grafted in: (a) from #2, harvesting the station's 19 named health conditions out of the health frame the ingest already caches — the largest new-signal-per-line item available — plus ack as a conditional UPDATE returning 409 with the current owner, which makes "nothing handled twice" a database property rather than a UI convention; (b) from #3, guarding audio by joining the TENANT'S OWN existing audio group, which I verified makes lease renewal work with zero new code because groups.stations_subscribed_to parses group names positionally (groups.py:142-157); (c) from #3's judge, a per-channel 30-second client-side replay ring, because a watch position with no "say again" is unusable and the Opus packets are already in the browser.

Fatal flaws fixed. #1's: an ODIN operator was authorised as the vendor root account — fixed with a real second axis on platform-org membership, landed in phase 1, including two privilege-escalation paths I verified in identity.py and authorization.py that neither the design nor the judge named. #2's: conditions and events double-raise one physical fault — fixed with a three-disposition rules table where conditions own anything with a condition twin. #3's: joining :telemetry per channel drags 1 Hz ADS-B and lets aircraft evict Opus from a 64-frame queue — fixed by never joining telemetry and deriving the talk light from audio arrival. Plus #1's ADS-B merge being accelerated onto the ingest loop, and suppression shipping two phases after the alerts it exists to silence.

## The spine

A fixed 12-column wall inside the existing .pdash shell (styles.css:3982-4009), which is already a correct ODIN frame: fixed inset:0, its own 14px reading type scale, sharing --topbar-h so the bar does not jump on view switch.

LEFT 3 COLUMNS: a permanently visible alert rail, queue-shaped per design #2 — oldest-unhandled first, two controls per row, unhandled-by-default. This resolves the wall-versus-queue tension by making the queue a rail rather than a competing tab, so the wall and the work list are one screen.

CENTRE 6 COLUMNS: one tile per station, worst-first via the existing stationRank (PlatformDashboard.tsx:41-46), so trouble is always top-left and a tile appearing there is caught peripherally, before anyone reads a word. At 2560x1440 that is ~48 tiles with no scroll — the whole current fleet.

RIGHT 3 COLUMNS: fleet map above, watch strip below. The watch strip is persistent across every ODIN surface, not a panel on one tab: for a dispatcher the watch is the shift and the alerts are the interruption, and burying the channels inside one tab inverts that.

Structurally ODIN is a fourth deliberately-scoped cross-tenant side channel built to the template of the three that already work (api/console.py, api/host.py, api/platform.py): platform gate, privileged station resolve, capped read-only capability set, audit written against the STATION's org with the operator as actor. It never widens capabilities_for (authorization.py:99-110) or visible_station_ids (:160-174) — both carry in-source post-mortems about exactly that widening — and it adds no primitive to groups.py.

The one enabling fact the surveys turned up and I re-verified in the source: a listening watch is nearly free. The SDR demodulates, squelches, records and transcribes unconditionally, and audio_wanted gates only the publish (agent.py:1643-1650), so guarding a channel costs that station ~30-36 kbit/s while its squelch is open and zero extra CPU or power. Video is the opposite (2-4 Mbit/s of metered satellite plus a standing encode, config.py:499-505). The whole design follows from that asymmetry: audio is always-on and cheap, video is one deliberate button.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — The wall, cold — and an operator who is not root

A real operations wall running on nothing but today's 15s poll and one Redis MGET, plus the authorisation split that makes ODIN staffable. No new socket, no new schema beyond one index. Ships standalone: the Stations table stops being an inventory list, the map stops being a picture, and 'how is that site doing' stops meaning 'open a root terminal on it'. Three items are worth shipping on their own merits (shared status derivation, the notable-events index, the unawaited unsubscribe).

The auth split is here rather than later because it is one column and a handful of guards now, versus a migration against a live rota later. VERIFIED PROBLEM: is_platform_admin is derived purely from the session's active org (auth/identity.py:123) with no role test anywhere, and platform-org membership is the ONLY gate on a root PTY (api/host.py:111), the station settings proxy (api/console.py:238), signed release publication (api/releases.py:90,121) and org/user CRUD. Without this, hiring a night-shift operator means handing them root on every customer's hardware.

2. auth/platform.py — require_platform_admin gains a role test: identity.is_platform_admin AND UserRole.ADMIN.value in identity.roles. Identity.roles is already the platform membership's own roles (auth/identity.py:130), and ensure_platform_admin.py:113-122 already writes ['admin'], so existing admins are unaffected. Add require_odin_watch: platform org active AND roles intersecting {admin, watch}.

Confirmed bug: the call is a no-op, the connection never leaves the group, and :129 then tells the client it worked. Latent only because Console.tsx never unsubscribes; it becomes load-bearing in phase 4.

Assert: a watch-role session gets 403 on the host-shell ticket, the console proxy, POST /api/releases and platform org/user CRUD; a watch-role session switching into a customer org receives NO admin role and no actuator capability; an existing admin's switch-into-tenant workflow still works; capabilities_for still returns the empty set cross-org. This suite gates the merge.

**Client**

1. components/OdinWall.tsx (new) — the default platform view, replacing the Overview tab content. Fixed 12-column grid inside .pdash. No drag-and-drop, no widget config: a wall works because the operator has learned one screen so thoroughly that a change in the corner registers pre-attentively, and a rearrangeable wall has no corners.
2. components/OdinStatusBar.tsx — one 56px line of tabular figures ('47 stations / 44 online / 2 offline / 1 DARK / 3 unacked') where only non-zero bad numbers take colour, UTC and local clocks, and the LIVENESS PIP. In phase 1 the pip ticks per completed poll and goes amber with an explicit 'polling (15s)' / 'stale 47s' label on failure. PlatformDashboard.tsx:89-125 currently swallows poll errors and renders a twenty-minute-old fleet with total confidence; a wall that degrades silently is a lie, and the operator's certainty peaks exactly when the data is worst.
3. components/StationTile.tsx — 6px status band as the colour carrier, station name >=15px for 3m legibility, org small beneath, SoC as a horizontal bar not a number (bars read at distance), four vitals glyphs (link / battery / radio with a lit block on squelch / camera), fault badge, and a decaying halo on any tile whose state changed in the last 60s. Tabular figures so a changing digit never reflows — reflow is motion and motion is the alarm channel.
4. components/TileWall.tsx — worst-first sort reusing stationRank (PlatformDashboard.tsx:41-46); past ~60 stations it paginates by EXCEPTION, collapsing nominal stations into one '38 nominal' block so only non-nominal stations keep a tile; hover cross-highlights the map.
5. components/AlertRail.tsx — read-only in phase 1, fed by the de-noised feed. Queue-shaped per design #2: unacked first, then severity, then oldest-first (a queue ages upward; newest-first is a social feed). Ages keyed on received_at, and rows whose clock is 'unsynced' are labelled rather than drawn confidently in the wrong hour (station_event.py:60-81).
6. components/StationPreviewDrawer.tsx (new) — slides over the right column. Never a modal, never a route change, never an org switch: the only path to a customer station today is UserMenu.tsx:68-77 -> switchOrganization -> window.location.reload(), which revokes the session (api/auth.py:455-467) and destroys the wall. Renders the full cached health projection, open faults and recent events. Esc closes; the wall stays lit behind it.
7. FleetMap.tsx:163-190 — add click handlers to the hand-built marker elements (the only affordance today is a DOM title at :186-189) plus selected/highlighted states. This is what turns the map from a picture into the wall's navigation surface.
8. Stations tab — add search, org grouping and the vitals columns. KEEP the Console and Shell buttons here (PlatformDashboard.tsx:393-421) as well as putting them in the drawer: moving them into an alert-driven drawer would leave a healthy station with no open alert unreachable, and these are the only two cross-tenant admin paths that exist. Both hidden entirely for the watch role.
9. Re-parent SettingsPlatform.tsx and SettingsAccount.tsx to a separate /admin route off UserMenu, admin-only. Two of four top-level tabs are currently org CRUD, user CRUD, the signed release catalogue and the admin's own password — occasional deliberate work at a completely different tempo, costing ODIN half its navigation. This is re-parenting working components, and it is only honest because the endpoints behind them are now genuinely admin-gated.
10. Wall CSS: near-black ground, one flat neutral for nominal, and exactly four accents — amber (warning/offline), red (critical/dark), gold (aircraft, already CONTACT_COLOUR in FleetMap), and green used ONLY for the liveness pip. Green never means 'this station is fine', because a wall of green trains you to ignore colour; fine is the absence of colour.

**Risk.** The auth split touches identity resolution for every session in the product, on a codebase with no automated authorisation tests. It must not merge without test_odin_isolation.py green in BOTH directions (escalation closed, admin support workflow intact), and a pre-deploy query confirming every platform membership row carries 'admin'. Secondary: widening FleetStation adds an MGET to the 15s poll — verify it stays fail-soft when Redis is down, or the wall goes blank on a Redis blip rather than degrading to liveness-only.

### Phase 2 — The wall, live

The wall stops polling and starts being pushed, and the cost model becomes O(1) sockets and O(1) frames per operator regardless of fleet size — the inverse of what today's primitives give you. Aircraft stop lagging at 6s, the fleet ADS-B merge stops being recomputed per viewer per request, and the liveness pip starts meaning something real. Worth having independently of alerting: it is the difference between a wall you trust and a wall you re-check by hand.

**Server**

1. realtime/odin.py (new) — the wall registry, deliberately OUTSIDE the Hub and modelled on realtime/media.py, which is the existing precedent for a fan-out registry that is not the hub. Consequences: an ODIN wall socket structurally cannot join an org: group, groups.py needs no new function, and these connections never enter the hub's per-connection revalidation sweep (which would otherwise add two blocking DB queries per operator per minute for nothing).
2. Per-viewer queue is depth-2 drop-oldest for digest frames, not the connection's 64-frame default (connection.py:18). A slow wall must get the NEWEST fleet state and drop the stale one, never accumulate a backlog: a wall that is behind is worse than a wall that skipped a frame.
3. api/odin.py (new) — /api/odin/ws, cookie-authenticated at connect (never a query-string token, endpoint.py:49-62), behind require_odin_watch. File header repeats api/platform.py:1-13's rule verbatim: this session bypasses RLS, so every query scopes itself in code.
4. services/odin_digest.py (new), hosted in the ingest leader so its single-instance property comes from the existing Redis lease (station_ingest.py:31-39) rather than a new assumption. Keeps a per-station vitals dict updated with an O(1) dict write on the ingest hot path at station_ingest.py:471-482 — no I/O added to a path that already publishes every frame from every station. A separate asyncio task joins it against a 30s-refreshed roster and publishes ONE frame to the odin:wall Redis channel every 3s. Computed once, not per viewer.
5. ADS-B merge: MOVE IT, BUT DO NOT ACCELERATE IT. A background task in the ingest process runs the existing merge (api/platform.py:769-831) at the SAME 6s cadence as today, inside asyncio.to_thread, writing latest:adsb:fleet with a 15s TTL; /api/platform/adsb reads that one key and falls back to the live merge when absent. Today the merge runs per viewer on FastAPI's sync threadpool; at 200 stations by up to 500 contacts, running it three times more often directly on the single-leader asyncio loop — the acknowledged fleet ceiling — would be strictly worse than doing nothing for a one-operator centre.
6. Beyond ~150 stations the digest switches to changed-stations-only with a full keyframe every 30s, deliberately the same shape as the media relay's GOP cache (realtime/media.py:68-69) so it is a pattern the team already reasons about.

**Client**

1. useOdin.ts (new) — the /api/odin/ws client, handling digest frames and reconciling them into the wall in place rather than rebuilding.
2. Automatic fallback to the existing 15s fleet and 6s ADS-B polls when the socket is down — and the fallback is LABELLED on the status bar, never silent. Degrading quietly to a poll is how a wall becomes a screensaver nobody notices has stopped.
3. The liveness pip switches from ticking per completed poll to ticking per digest frame.

**Risk.** The digest's dict write sits on the ingest hot path, which already carries every frame from every station through one asyncio loop. Keep it a pure dict assignment with no serialisation and no I/O; do the join and the JSON encode in the 3s publisher task. Measure the ingest loop on the bench at 200 simulated stations before and after — that loop, not bandwidth, is the honest ceiling.

### Phase 3 — Alerts that exist, and can be silenced

An alert becomes a row in the database, which it has never been. Today every alert in the product is a React object in one browser tab, capped at 40 and dead on reload (Console.tsx:219,:289-300), and the platform side has nothing beyond a bare ORDER BY received_at DESC LIMIT 20. After this phase an alert survives a restart, a reload and a shift change; two operators cannot handle the same thing twice; and a known-bad site can be told to stop shouting. Suppression ships in this phase, not later — a design whose stated biggest risk is alert fatigue must not schedule its own primary mitigation after the risk arrives.

The single largest coverage gain is the station's 19 named health conditions (station/gsu/health.py:43-119), which today exist only inside a live telemetry frame and reach the vendor nowhere at all: credential.renewal_failing can be true for six hours and leave no queryable trace. They are already in the blob the ingest caches — the ingest's own bounds check admits conditions as a list of up to 64 — so this is a diff on data already in flight, with no contract change and no station change.

**Server**

1. Migration 0019 — platform_alerts (organization_id = the STATION's org, ground_station_id, source, dedupe_key, type, severity, title, message, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, occurrences, state, acked_by_user_id, acked_at, ack_note, closed_at, closed_reason, snooze_until), with RLS + FORCE copied from 0015_station_events_rls.py:44-47. Severity stays info|warning|critical — the contract vocabulary is fixed and deliberately shared between conditions and events; do not invent a fourth level.
2. Two indexes carry the design: UNIQUE (ground_station_id, dedupe_key) WHERE state <> 'closed' — one open alert per fact, so a re-raise bumps occurrences and cannot stack, which also makes the raise path idempotent under at-least-once redelivery (station_event.py:87-99). And (state, severity, first_seen_at) WHERE state <> 'closed' — the rail reads a hot set of hundreds, never the transcript-dominated event table.
3. Migration 0019 also adds station_maintenance (ground_station_id, from_at, until_at, reason, created_by). Suppression ships WITH the engine.
4. services/alerts/rules.py — a ~40-line dict with THREE dispositions per type, which is the fix for the verified double-raise. CONDITION-OWNED: the station raises a health condition AND records an event for one physical fact on adjacent lines (agent.py:1916/1921 power.battery, :1991-2003 light.no_draw/stuck_on, :2071/2076 uplink.down), and only the condition self-clears — so the condition is the authority for power.battery, light.*, uplink.down, clock.implausible, credential.*, enrolment.*, tls.*. EVIDENCE: the paired event for a condition-owned fault bumps occurrences and appends to the alert's timeline, and NEVER raises. That is critical, because the matching recovery events (power.recovered agent.py:1911, uplink.up :2066, light.recovered :2009) are all severity 'info', so a deny-info policy guarantees an event-raised alert could never close itself and the morning shift inherits duplicate rows for faults that already fixed themselves overnight. EVENT-OWNED: genuinely occurrence-shaped types with no condition twin — video.stream_*, update failures, and any unknown type at warning/critical, so the policy degrades gracefully on a vocabulary the station owns. NEVER AN ALERT: radio.transmission and adsb.proximity.
5. services/alerts/store.py — open_or_touch / ack / close / snooze / auto_close, all idempotent. Ack is UPDATE ... SET state='acked', acked_by=:user WHERE id=:id AND state='open' RETURNING *; zero rows means somebody else took it, so return 409 naming the current owner and let the client re-render rather than retry. Ack IS assignment — no separate assignee concept and no 'assigned' state. One concept, learned once.
6. services/alert_engine.py in the ingest leader. The condition diff is taken against the PREVIOUS value of latest:health:{id}, read immediately before the overwrite in _cache_health (station_ingest.py:561-577) — NOT a SELECT against platform_alerts per frame. At 200 stations health lands ~7/s and a per-frame DB read would sit squarely on the single-leader loop. Raise on the appearing edge, auto-close on the disappearing edge, mirroring Console.tsx:481-502's {station}:{condition.id} raise/forget semantics server-side and durably.
7. services/station_watch.py:109 — replace the in-process `alerted` set with open_or_touch(source='dark'). This deletes the confirmed bug that every deploy re-announces every currently-dark station as new. Also write a platform.station.dark row into station_events using the reserved, defended and entirely unused platform. prefix (services/station_events.py:57-79) — this is what it was reserved for.
8. api/broker.py:248-263 — feed debounced connect/disconnect into the engine. This is the fastest and most trustworthy liveness signal in the system and is currently fanned out live and never recorded. Debounced, because an ordinary Starlink obstruction must not raise an alert.
9. api/odin.py — GET /alerts (state/severity/org/station/since), POST /alerts/{id}/ack, /close, /snooze, POST /alerts/ack-all with an explicit required filter (03:00 after a comms outage must be one action), POST /stations/{id}/maintenance. Every ack and every suppression writes an audit row against the STATION's organisation with the operator as actor, copying api/console.py:314-331.
10. api/platform.py — faults_24h now counts platform_alerts, which is O(alerts) rather than O(total fleet event volume) per request per operator.

**Client**

1. AlertRail becomes a work surface: one-click ack with no modal and no confirm. Ack and close are deliberately different — ack means 'I have seen it', close means 'it stopped being true' — and a station keeps its attention colour until closed, not until acked. Conflating those two is how command centres lose faults.
2. Alerts acked by another operator repaint within one frame off the odin:wall channel, so two people at two desks cannot duplicate work.
3. chime.ts:15-43 rewired from ADS-B proximity only (Console.tsx:449, which is why a critical credential.revoked currently arrives in total silence) to unacked critical only, two-tone, rate-limited to one chime per 10s regardless of volume.
4. Unacked count written into document.title so a second-monitor operator sees it.
5. Maintenance/suppression toggle in the preview drawer, with the window and reason shown on the tile so a suppressed station reads as deliberately quiet rather than healthy.

**Risk.** Alert fatigue is the failure mode that kills these tools, and it fails silently — a rail operators have stopped reading gives no signal that it has stopped working. The type filter (phase 1), one-open-alert-per-key, the chime rate limit, ack-all and maintenance windows must all be in place before the engine turns on. Adopt the standing rule that adding an alert source requires deleting or filtering a noise source.

### Phase 4 — The listening watch

The headline operational requirement: an operator guards up to 8 airband channels across multiple sites and organisations simultaneously, dispatcher-style, with per-channel activity, panning, priority ducking, instant replay and a live transcript companion. Verified cost: ~30-36 kbit/s per guarded station and only while its squelch is open, with zero extra station CPU or power. Note the platform docstrings quoting 512 kbit/s (audio_demand.py:1-10, hub.py:199-201, agent.py:1643-1648) are pre-Opus and wrong by ~15x against the measured 21.7 kbit/s (opus.py:126-140) — correcting them is a line item, because a capacity argument built on them would wrongly reject this feature as unaffordable.

**Server**

1. auth/odin.py (new) — ODIN_READ = {station.view, telemetry.view, radio.listen, video.view}, a strict subset of READ_CAPABILITIES (capabilities.py:75-81) containing no actuator, ever; and odin_capabilities_for(db, station_id) resolving the station on a privileged session and returning the set only if is_active. Deliberately does NOT touch capabilities_for or visible_station_ids. This is the first code that enforces docs/03-realtime-isolation.md section 9's documented read-only ceiling, which today exists only as prose.
2. realtime/connection.py — one new field, watch: set[uuid.UUID] = field(default_factory=set). Do NOT touch station_id or the one-station pin: select_station drops the previous station's groups in the SAME operation so the property is true at every instant (hub.py:167-172), and that is what leaves the hot path with no auth decision to get wrong.
3. realtime/hub.py — watch_join(conn, station_id) / watch_leave(...). Resolves the station's org from the station row on a PRIVILEGED session, never from client input, and joins ONLY station_group(station_org, station_id, 'audio') — the tenant's own existing group.
4. DO NOT JOIN :telemetry. The telemetry group is undifferentiated — station_ingest.py:451-482 publishes adsb, power, radio, light, weather and health to one stream and hub.py:36-40 has no per-kind granularity — so joining it for a squelch lamp drags that site's full ADS-B at 1 Hz, which contract/transport.md:452-455 prices at 68-153 kbit/s CONTINUOUSLY against 30-36 kbit/s of squelch-gated Opus. Worse, with SEND_QUEUE_MAX=64 drop-oldest (connection.py:18) twelve channels is ~50-60 frames/s, so under any stall aircraft positions evict Opus packets and the operator hears a clipped over indistinguishable from a quiet channel — guarding more channels would silently make the radio worse.
5. THE TALK LIGHT IS FREE FROM AUDIO ARRIVAL. The station builds an audio payload only while the gate is open and publishes it only when audio_wanted (agent.py:1643-1650), so a frame arriving on a channel IS the gate, at 125 ms granularity, with no telemetry subscription at all. Frequency and label come from the 30s roster snapshot.
6. LEASE RENEWAL NEEDS ZERO NEW CODE — verified. groups.stations_subscribed_to (groups.py:142-157) parses group NAMES positionally and returns station ids, so a watch connection sitting in the tenant's real audio group is counted by audio_demand.renew (audio_demand.py:82) automatically, with no union, no second registry and no new lease path. Also call audio_demand.request(station_id) on join so the first over is not missed waiting up to RENEW_SECONDS, following hub.py:207-210.
7. REVOCATION MUST REACH A WATCH CONNECTION. hub.connections_for_station:108-116 matches c.station_id == station_id or station_id in c.visible_stations; a watch connection has station_id=None and an empty visible set in the platform org, so a tenant deactivating a station would NOT push-stop a cross-tenant listen — it would wait up to 60s for the sweep. Add `or station_id in c.watch`, and give connections_for_organization (:105) a branch matching the org of any watched station. For the one feature that crosses tenant boundaries by design, the tenant-side stop levers are precisely the ones that currently miss.
8. hub._revalidate_one:304-327 — add a watch branch: re-check the session is live and the caller still holds admin/watch in the platform org, and resolve the WHOLE guard set in ONE statement (WHERE id = ANY(:ids) AND is_active) rather than per station. It currently returns early when station_id is None (:320-321), so a watch connection would never be re-checked at all; and per-station reads would multiply the already-serial 60s sweep (hub.py:358-368) by the guard count.
9. realtime/odin.py — /odin/watch speaking guard {station_id} / unguard {station_id}, where the client's guard set REPLACES rather than adds on reconnect, matching the leases-replace-never-extend instinct the station already enforces (station/gsu/stream.py:14-17). Every join writes an audit row against the STATION's org with the operator as actor (services/audit.py:27-58) — so 'who was listening to Kennels Road at 03:12' finally has an answer.
10. api/odin.py — GET /transcripts, a fleet-wide merged radio.transmission feed with a cursor on received_at and org/station/text filters. Nearly free: the rows are already in the table ODIN queries, and today the only read path over the ledger is hardcoded to one station and one type (api/stations.py:496-501).
11. Correct the three stale 512 kbit/s docstrings (audio_demand.py:1-10, hub.py:199-201, agent.py:1643-1648).

**Client**

1. useWatchAudio.ts — the multi-channel refactor of useAudio.ts. ONE shared AudioContext (Chrome caps concurrent contexts per document at around six, which would cap the headline feature below the requirement before bandwidth ever did), with per channel a decoder, a GainNode and a StereoPannerNode into a master gain. Lift verbatim: the AudioWorklet-vs-scheduled-source fallback, which exists because AudioWorklet is secure-context-only and plain-HTTP LAN deploys have no ctx.audioWorklet at all (useAudio.ts:30-36); BURST_LEAD_S=0.3; flush-on-retune; and the carried resampler phase and tail (:246-270), which becomes PER-CHANNEL state and is the single most likely place to reintroduce the 44.1 kHz chopping bug.
2. Keep useAudio.ts:199-216's rule that audio starts only from the volume control and never from a page click. An operator who opens the wall to check a battery level should not get sound because they clicked a map — that decision matters more in a shared operations room, not less.
3. Fixed stereo pan in guard-list order, so position on screen equals position in the ears and an operator learns 'the one on the left is Kennels Road'. Priority ducking of -12 dB over 80ms via gain.setTargetAtTime when a higher-priority channel opens; a single PRI flag per channel, not a priority integer.
4. PER-CHANNEL 30-SECOND REPLAY RING. A watch position with no 'say again' is unusable, the transcript lags an over by seconds to tens of seconds, and the station's own disk recording is unreachable from the wall. The Opus packets are already in the browser, so this needs no server surface, no storage argument and no new authorisation.
5. components/WatchStrip.tsx — persistent across every ODIN surface, hard cap of 8 channels (a human limit, not a bandwidth one). Label is always 'Kennels Road @ 118.900', never a bare frequency, because one station is one channel. The wide squelch-open activity bar is the peripheral element: on airband the interesting question is WHICH channel is talking, and that must be answerable without reading.
6. Saved watch sets in localStorage plus a one-action 'load shift watch'. An operator coming on at 22:00 should load their channels in one click, not eight.
7. 'Add to watch' from the alert drawer and from a transcript row; clicking a transcript row guards that station and scrolls its strip into view.
8. components/TranscriptFeed.tsx in the drawer — the text companion to the audio, tinted to match each guarded channel's strip.

**Risk.** This is the cross-tenant leak surface, and it is guarded entirely by hand-written code. The org id in the joined group name must be resolved from the privileged station row and never from a request body; a future refactor that lets it come from client input puts a connection into a group it was never authorised into, and the publish path performs no authorisation at all by design (hub.py:222-251). Extend test_odin_isolation.py before this ships: a watch connection never lands in an org status group, unguard actually leaves the group, deactivating a station push-stops the listen, and revocation clears the whole guard set.

### Phase 5 — Deliberate reach, and what a month of running teaches

The one-station-at-a-time deep look, plus the hygiene ODIN's own load forces. Worth having independently: it closes the loop from 'I heard something odd on that channel' to 'I can see that site', and it stops both fleet queries degrading monotonically with age rather than with fleet size.

**Server**

1. api/odin.py — POST /stations/{id}/attach: a single-use, 60s, station-bound ticket in the api/media.py:82-112 shape, with a privileged station resolve in the api/console.py:203-217 shape, re-checked at redemption rather than trusted from issue, and audited against the tenant's org. Grants ODIN_READ and no actuator, ever. This is the only sanctioned exception to endpoint.py:49-62's no-credential-in-a-query-string rule.
2. Live per-station telemetry into the drawer via the attach ticket — the preview card upgrades in place from the 30s snapshot to 1 Hz for the ONE selected station, which is the correct place to spend a telemetry subscription.
3. api/stations.py:391-399 — widen the health projection, which currently discards conditions, devices, video, storage, clock, security, cadence and position from a blob already in memory.
4. api/odin.py — GET /events: the general filterable event browser the ledger has never had (org, station, type, severity, time range), so 'show me every credential and uplink event for this station in the last 48 hours' is answerable.
5. A read API over audit_logs, filtered by org in hand-written code because audit_logs sits deliberately outside RLS (migration 0002:20-31 calls it out as the one table where a missing filter leaks cross-org). A centre that can open a root shell on a customer's station must be able to show who did that and when.
6. services/event_retention.py — a type-aware prune on the power_history.py:44 pattern: ~30-90 days for info-severity rows (the transcripts, which are the growth), 400 days for warning/critical and platform.*. station_events is the only such table with no retention today, and it carries every transcribed airband transmission fleet-wide forever.

**Client**

1. The deliberate video button in the drawer: 'Live video (uses uplink)', one station at a time, auto-stopping after 5 minutes, reusing useVideoStream.ts unchanged including its 30s buffer trim (:97-109, sized from a real measurement at :90-95 — ~3 Mbit/s hits Chrome's SourceBuffer quota in 4-5 minutes without it). Never on a tile, never a grid.
2. Convert the FleetMap aircraft overlay to a GeoJSON source with a symbol layer. 200 station DOM markers are fine; fleet-merged ADS-B across 200 receivers is thousands of DOM nodes reconciled on a timer, and MapLibre DOM markers do not cluster. Leave station markers as DOM — they need labels and click targets.
3. Alert notes and a shift-handover marker on the rail; MTTA/MTTR read straight out of platform_alerts, since the timestamps have been recorded since phase 3.

**Risk.** Concurrent video is what actually forces WEB_CONCURRENCY off 1 — the media relay, the stream/host-shell ticket dicts and the console proxy are in-process by design (scripts/start_app.py:135-159). Keeping video to one deliberate stream per operator is what lets the single worker stand; if the product later wants a mosaic, moving the relays out of process is a separate project that must land first.

## Decisions

### 1. Is an ODIN operator a different person from a vendor platform admin — i.e. will anyone ever sit the watch who should NOT have root on customer hardware?

- Yes — build the watch role in phase 1 (adds roughly two days: one enum value, two guard functions, two escalation fixes, a guard split across four files, and the isolation suite)
- No — ODIN is only ever operated by the two or three engineers who already hold platform admin; skip the role work and keep require_platform_admin everywhere

*Recommendation:* Yes, build it in phase 1. Platform-org membership is currently the ONLY gate on a root PTY into any customer's station (api/host.py:111), the settings proxy (api/console.py:238), publishing a signed image to the entire fleet (api/releases.py:121), and user/org CRUD. It is one column and four guards now; after a rota exists it is a migration against live sessions plus an access review. Note it is not purely additive: two verified escalation paths (auth/identity.py:98-105 and auth/authorization.py:60-70) currently hand ADMIN in any customer org to anyone merely holding a platform membership row, so both must be closed in the same change or the role is decorative.

*Why it matters:* It decides whether ODIN can be staffed by the people it is designed for. If the answer is no, phase 1 loses about two days and the plan is otherwise unchanged — but the decision is effectively irreversible in cost terms, because retrofitting it once operators exist means auditing what every existing account did.

**DECIDED 2026-08-14: yes — build the split in phase 1.** Odin will be staffed by people who must not hold root on customer hardware.

### 2. Does a customer know, or consent, when the vendor listens to their airband receiver or opens their camera?

- Silent — the audit trail is the whole control (fastest; ODIN as designed)
- Disclosed — the tenant's own console shows a 'vendor monitoring' indicator while a watch is held, and the audit log becomes readable by org admins
- Opt-in — a per-organisation flag that must be on before ODIN can guard that org's radio or attach video at all

*Recommendation:* Disclosed. It costs one status frame on the tenant's existing org channel plus the audit read API already scheduled in phase 5, and it is the only option that survives a customer asking the question after the fact. Hold opt-in in reserve for a customer who demands it, since the flag is cheap to add once the capability tier exists.

*Why it matters:* It is a contractual question wearing a UI costume, and the honest engineering position is that an audit trail makes cross-tenant listening accountable but not disclosed. If the answer is disclosed or opt-in it changes phase 4's scope — a tenant-facing indicator, an org flag checked at watch_join — and it must be decided before the tier is built, not after operators are using it.

### 3. Is on-box airband transcription turned on fleet-wide?

- Yes, fleet-wide — the talk log and the fleet transcript feed cover every station
- Selected stations only — the log is honestly partial and the UI must say which sites are not transcribing
- No change — transcripts stay a per-site opt-in and the transcript features are cut from phases 4 and 5

*Recommendation:* Selected stations, expanding after a CPU and power measurement on the Pi 5. GSU_RADIO_TRANSCRIBE is off by default (station/gsu/config.py:449), and turning it on fleet-wide is a real per-box CPU and solar-budget decision, not a platform setting.

*Why it matters:* The transcript feed is one of the cheapest features in the plan because radio.transmission rows already land in the table ODIN queries — but it is only as wide as the stations with transcription enabled. If the answer is 'no change', the fleet transcript feed and the per-channel transcript lines come out of phases 4 and 5, and the talk log stops being a navigation surface. It also sets the growth rate of station_events, which drives the retention work in phase 5.

### 4. Is the product owner's picture of the listening watch several SITES on one channel each, or several FREQUENCIES at one site?

- Several sites, one channel each — matches the hardware; build as planned
- Several frequencies at one site — requires a second SDR and a second receiver instance per site, which the station's slot model does not currently allow

*Recommendation:* Confirm 'several sites' before the guard list is designed. There is one device per slot and one RtlSdrFrontEnd per station (station/gsu/devices/registry.py:19-24,:35), so a channel is a station, and the UI must label it 'Kennels Road @ 118.900' rather than a bare frequency.

*Why it matters:* If the mental picture is six frequencies at one airfield, the answer is hardware and a station-side slot-model change, not a platform feature — and it should be corrected before anyone designs a UI around it, not discovered during the phase 4 review.

**DECIDED 2026-08-14: several sites, one channel each.** A channel is a station, so the listening watch is a pure platform feature with no station-side work.

### 5. Must an alert reach a human who is not looking at a screen — a station going dark at 03:00 with nobody at the console?

- Not in v1 — the wall, the chime and the tab-title count are the whole notification story
- One transport in phase 3 — email to a rota address for any critical unacked for N minutes, over the existing email service already wired for password reset
- Full routing — per-org rules, escalation, on-call rotas, paging integration

*Recommendation:* One transport, added at the END of phase 3 once the engine has run for a week and you can see which alerts actually fire and how often. Routing noise to a phone is worse than not routing at all.

*Why it matters:* It determines whether ODIN is a staffed-hours tool or an unattended one, which changes both the alerting scope and the staffing model. Full routing is a separate product — rules, escalation and delivery — and would displace the listening watch by roughly a phase.

## Explicitly not included

- A video mosaic or camera tile wall. Nine 1080p previews is ~27 Mbit/s of metered satellite plus nine standing ffmpeg encodes on solar-powered boxes (station/gsu/config.py:499-505) and nine SourceBuffers in one tab, each marching toward Chrome's quota in 4-5 minutes. Twenty audio channels is under ~1 Mbit/s and costs the stations no CPU at all. Video is one station, opened deliberately, closed by its lease — never on a tile.
- A cheap periodic-JPEG thumbnail tier to make a mosaic affordable. Technically available for RTSP stations (rtsp.py:16-22 records that two readers of a network camera do not contend), but the channel was deliberately deleted because two readers of one CSI sensor wedged the camera, and it is not worth the risk for a feature already rejected on other grounds.
- Radio tuning from ODIN. radio.control is excluded from ODIN_READ. Retuning is org-wide and deliberately unarbitrated (capabilities.py:31-41), so a vendor operator would silently change what a customer's own operators are hearing. If it is ever wanted it is a separate, audited, confirm-dialogged feature.
- Transmit in any form. radio.transmit is refused at the write boundary and org admins are explicitly not an exception (capabilities.py:56-70, authorization.py:112-118). ODIN adds no transmit surface.
- Any actuator at all cross-tenant — no light.set, no PTZ, no config.write, no station.update from the ODIN surface. ODIN_READ is a strict subset of READ_CAPABILITIES by construction, and the phase 1 auth split exists precisely so an operator cannot reach these by another door.
- Widening capabilities_for (authorization.py:73-136) or visible_station_ids (:142-194). Both carry in-source post-mortems describing the exact bug that widening reintroduces — a platform admin who ended up with five stations from two other tenants in their switcher. ODIN gets new functions in new files with a hard read-only ceiling.
- Any broadcast primitive in realtime/groups.py. No broadcast_all, no registry iteration, no debug dump. The wall's fan-out is a separate registry outside the Hub modelled on realtime/media.py, so an ODIN wall socket structurally cannot end up in an org: group; the watch path joins named tenant groups through an explicit privileged authorisation decision.
- Relaxing the one-station-per-connection pin on the customer console. hub.select_station drops the previous station's groups in the same operation so the property is true at every instant (hub.py:167-172); ODIN adds a separate endpoint and a separate guard set rather than making that invariant conditional.
- N WebSockets, one per guarded station. Forty sockets per operator means forty register() visibility queries and a serial 60s sweep doing two blocking DB queries per socket forever (hub.py:304-327,:358-368). One socket with a guard set resolved in a single statement.
- Org switching as the drill-in path. It mints a new session, revokes the old one, closes every socket and reloads the page (api/auth.py:455-467, UserMenu.tsx:68-77) — the wall is gone to look at one site. Drill-in is a drawer.
- A customisable dashboard — draggable panels, per-operator widget layouts, saved views. It is the first thing anyone asks for and it destroys the only property that matters: a wall works because an operator has learned one screen so thoroughly that a change in the corner registers pre-attentively. A rearrangeable wall has no corners.
- A six-KPI tile row. It costs 180px to say less than a 56px status line, and six large numbers with no context are exactly what eyes learn to skip.
- An alert rules engine with user-authored thresholds and a UI. Every threshold that matters already lives on the station and must, because it has to work with the platform unreachable (station/gsu/config.py:441-457). ODIN surfaces what the station concluded; it does not re-derive it. Rules are a ~40-line dict somebody can read.
- On-call rotas, escalation policies and paging integrations. At most one outbound transport (see decisions), and not before the engine has run long enough to show what actually fires.
- A separate assignee concept. Ack is ownership, enforced by a conditional UPDATE. Two concepts where one will do is how a queue stops being read.
- Fleet-scoped station grants. The grant model deliberately refuses an org-wide wildcard so 'who can see station 7' is one query with no inference; a fleet operator would otherwise need a row per station per org maintained forever.
- Sharing acknowledgement state with the customer console. The tenant's own alert drawer is unchanged. Telling a customer 'we have acknowledged this' is a contractual promise, not a UI feature.
- New telemetry fields or any contract change. Contract 2.1 is frozen and additive-only; everything ODIN needs is a platform-side derivation of what the station already sends.
- Persisted history for ADS-B, radio state or health. That is new storage rather than a new query, and none of it is needed to watch a wall. Power and weather already have 31-day history; transcripts already persist as events.
- Platform-side storage of airband audio. The station already records every over to local disk regardless of any lease (agent.py:1616-1640); the transcript is the searchable artefact and the 30-second replay ring lives in the browser.
- Moving the media relay, ticket stores and console proxy out of process to lift WEB_CONCURRENCY=1. Real work, correctly deferred — ODIN's audio, alert and digest paths all cross Redis and are unaffected, and it is the video wall this design refuses to build that would have forced it.
- Any DJI dock surface, dock data model, or dock alerting. Out of scope by product decision; nothing in this plan touches it.

## Biggest technical risk

The phase 1 authorisation change is the highest-blast-radius edit in the plan, and it lands on the one area of this codebase with no automated tests at all.

It rewrites who counts as an admin of what, for every session in the product: require_platform_admin gains a role test, and the two bare-membership tests at auth/identity.py:98-105 and auth/authorization.py:60-70 — which currently mint roles=(ADMIN,) in ANY customer org for anyone holding a platform membership row — must both change in the same commit. Get it half-right in one direction and the watch role is decorative, because an operator simply switches into a tenant and comes back as its admin with light.control, radio.control, config.write and station.update. Get it half-right in the other direction and every existing platform admin loses cross-org support access on deploy, including the org switcher and the guest-identity path that lets them work inside a tenant at all. There is no staging tenant with real stations to catch the second failure.

Mitigations: both call sites in one commit; app/tests/test_odin_isolation.py green before merge, asserting BOTH that the escalation is closed AND that an existing admin's switch-into-tenant workflow still works; a pre-deploy query confirming every platform membership row carries 'admin' (ensure_platform_admin.py:113-122 writes it, but a hand-created row may not); and the guard split verified by walking all 24 require_platform_admin call sites rather than by grep-and-hope — api/tiles.py:150,168 in particular is easy to miss and its only symptom is a blank fleet map for exactly the new role.

The close second is a cross-tenant leak through phase 4's watch_join, where the joined group name embeds another tenant's org id. groups.py:16-21 warns in as many words that the wrong shape here "would look entirely reasonable in review", and the publish path performs no authorisation whatsoever by design (hub.py:222-251) because membership IS the permission. Mitigations are structural rather than careful: the org id is resolved from the privileged station row and never from client input; the watch path never joins an org status group, so the per-recipient visibility filter at hub.py:236-247 is never the thing standing between two tenants; the wall registry lives outside the Hub entirely; and the revocation gaps at hub.py:105 and :108-116 are closed in the same phase, because today a tenant deactivating a station would not push-stop a cross-tenant listen.

The non-technical risk worth naming alongside them, because it fails silently and permanently: the wall becoming wallpaper, through alert fatigue or through a silent freeze. A leak is a bug you find and fix; a screen operators have stopped looking at gives no signal that it has stopped working. Both mechanisms are addressed in the design rather than as follow-ups — type filtering before alerts exist at all, suppression shipping with the engine, one open alert per key, a chime rate limit, and the liveness pip plus labelled degradation plus a depth-2 digest queue.
